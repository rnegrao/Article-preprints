# Experimental evidences of ⁹Be breakup to ⁸Be + n channel

(Dated: January 18, 2010)

---

## Abstract

In this work we performed α–α particle coincidence measurements from the ⁹Be + ¹²C nuclear reaction at \( E_{\text{Lab}} = 21 \) MeV in order to obtain information about the breakup \( ^9\text{Be} \rightarrow ^8\text{Be} + n \) channel. From systematic analysis of the ⁸Be energy spectrum we see two different situations: at high energy side, there is indication that neutron transfer from ⁹Be to ¹²C is the main reaction channel, while at low energies, a continuum background might be related to ⁹Be going through nuclear breakup process.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Interest in nuclear reactions involving weakly bound nuclei and unstable nuclei have increased in the last few years due to the fact that in such systems transfer [1,2] and nuclear breakup [3–6] play an important role in the reaction dynamics. In order to understand these processes provide valuable information about the nuclear structure, such as single-particle states and cluster structure, as well as about the influence of continuum states [7] on the reaction dynamics.

Moreover, the competition between breakup and fusion channels, involving stable light weakly bound nuclei like ⁹Be, ⁶Li and ⁷Li, has become subject of current interest [7–12]. The fusion phenomenon is an important physical process closely related with the superheavy element production, as well as in the generation of energy in an interstellar medium through nucleosynthesis processes. With this motivation, light weakly bound systems are ideal to test theoretical models, pushing them to limits of applicability, becoming important to increase our understanding about these nuclei.

With the aim of understanding the influence of breakup channel on fusion, recent experimental works adopted the strategy to compare experimental fusion cross sections with realistic calculations without couplings to breakup channel [12–14]. Differently, other strategy is to measure directly the breakup products, and therefore coincidence measurements of particles produced in the reaction are necessary to disentangle the contribution of many different reaction mechanisms.

Particularly for the ⁹Be nucleus, the breakup processes can proceed through ⁹Be → ⁸Be + n (Q = −1.66) or ⁹Be → α + ⁵He (Q = 2.467) channels, where in the first case the neutron separation energy is smaller than the α-cluster separation. In more detail, in the first case due ⁸Be instability to α–α channel decay, the reconstruction by α–α coincidence measurement is required to achieve information about this breakup channel.

Therefore, in the present work we performed α–α particles coincidence measurements for the ⁹Be + ¹²C nuclear reaction at \( E_{\text{Lab}} = 21 \) MeV, in order to through the α–α reconstruction process identify the ⁹Be breakup channel.

One experimental difficulty encountered in such studies is that neutron transfer reactions can produce a couple of alpha particles equally to breakup reactions [15]. It will be clearer in the next sections, the results from the ⁸Be reconstruction analysis show a strong signature of neutron transfer reaction from ⁹Be to single particle states of ¹³C. In the low energy side the ⁸Be energy spectrum shows a continuum structure typically associated with three body reactions [16]. To identify the reactions channels present we compare the experimental data with kinematics solutions considering the neutron transfer channel ⁹Be + ¹²C → ⁸Be + ¹³C and breakup channel ⁹Be + ¹²C → ⁸Be + n + ¹²C.

## II. EXPERIMENTAL DESCRIPTION AND DATA

The experiment was performed at the University of São Paulo Physics Institute. The ⁹Be beam was delivered by 8UD Pelletron accelerator with energies \( E_{\text{Lab}} = 21 \) MeV and bombarded a 40 μg/cm² thick ¹²C target.

Charged particles from ⁹Be + ¹²C reaction were detected by a set of 18 triple telescopes detector [1]. Each telescope detector is composed of three parts: an ionization chamber, filled with isobutane gas at 20 torr, a 150 μm silicon detector and a 40 mm CsI scintillator crystal. The electronics associated to each detector was mounted to allow for temporal coincidence measurements and, in this way, making possible measurements of α particles from the ⁸Be decay illustrated in Fig. 1.

![Figure 1: Schematic view of experimental setup.](figure1.png)

In Fig. 2-a is shown a typical alpha particle energy spectrum obtained from one detector. It is observed peaks at high energies due to single particle states in the residual nucleus. The continuum background at low energies is interpreted as compound nucleus formation followed by α particle evaporation and incomplete fusion where one of the α particles produced in breakup process are absorbed.

To separate the (⁹Be,⁸Be) channel from other reaction mechanisms with contribution to alpha particles production, it is necessary to distinguish α particles of ⁸Be decay from that produced by other reaction mechanisms. In order to accomplish that, α–α coincidence was performed among all detectors, e.g., at a given event it is identified the alpha particle in one detector and looked for another correlated alpha particle in the other 17 detectors. Those α particles in coincidence to another one are assigned to originated from the ⁸Be decay.

To illustrate the effects of the coincidence conditions on the α particle yield we show in Fig. 2-b a typical energy spectrum for α particles in coincidence. At this time we removed the background seen in Fig. 2-a and showed up well defined peaks and a broad peak at low energy.

![Figure 2: Energy distribution for (a) non correlated α particles and (b) correlated α particles.](figure2.png)

In order to obtain more information about the α particles decay, a comparison between experimental data and kinematics correlation considering a ground state ⁸Be decay are made. The results are presented in Fig. 3-a, where a good agreement of experimental data with calculated values confirms that those α particles comes from (⁹Be,⁸Be) channel. Indeed in Fig. 3-b we show the relative energy between the α particles calculated from the experimental data. The spectrum show a peak around 0.087 MeV, which is very close to 0.092 MeV for ⁸Be ground state decay. A slight background was observed in this spectrum indicating no problems in the coincidence-α particles identification process.

![Figure 3: (a) Biparametric spectrum for correlated α particles. The line curve represents the kinematic calculation for ⁸Be ground state decay. (b) Relative energy between correlated α particles.](figure3.png)

The experimental results support a strong indication to ⁹Be = n + ⁸Be(0⁺) cluster configuration in the (⁹Be,⁸Be) reactions channel due the weakly binding energy of neutron on ⁸Be core. To investigate the ⁹Be = α + ⁵He breakup channel, a kinematic simulation is made and from the results we have indication that the slight background observed in detail in the insert on Fig. 3-b are events originated from ⁹Be = α + ⁵He channel. No events from ⁹Be = n + ⁸Be(2⁺) are measured.

## III. DATA ANALYSIS AND DISCUSSION

In previous section we discussed how to separate the coincident α particles to access the (⁹Be,⁸Be) channel events. In order, in this section we will discuss the results from the ⁸Be originated nucleus reconstructed through these α particles.

In Fig. 4, we show the ⁸Be energy as function of the scattering angle in the laboratory frame, measure in all pairs of detectors. In the graph we compare the experimental data with the kinematics calculations considering the neutrons transfer from the ⁹Be to ¹²C producing ¹³C in the ground state, 1/2⁺ and 5/2⁺, and the breakup channel. The calculated values show a good agreement with the experimental data, supporting the conclusions that the peaks at high energy side are originated in neutron transfer reactions to single particle state of ¹³C in complete agreement with previous work [15].

In the low energy side the continuum are compatible with the breakup channel kinematic area.

In more details, the ⁸Be energy spectrum to a fix angle is shown in Fig. 5 where we see the same structure as it was shown in the Fig. 3-b to α particles measure. Like explained before, the peaks present in high energy side are very closed to neutron transfer reaction mechanism and in the low energy side a broad peak typically produced by three body reaction channel. In this region we can see low yield peaks maybe associated with high energy states of ¹³C.

![Figure 5: ⁸Be energy spectrum for a fix angle.](figure5.png)

With the analysis of the ⁸Be reconstructed energy spectrum we can see two different situations. The first at high energies, there are isolated peaks related with single particle states in the ¹³C residual nucleus, and the second at low energies, a continuum region related with three bodies reaction compatible with breakup process.

Through the neutrons transfer yield, from ⁹Be to ground state, 1/2⁺ and 5/2⁺ of ¹³C, we can conclude that the neutron transfer process is more favorable than breakup.

---

## IV. CONCLUSIONS

In summary we have performed α–α particle coincidence measurements for the ⁹Be + ¹²C nuclear reaction at \( E_{\text{Lab}} = 21 \) MeV. From the kinematics analysis, the experimental results support a strong component to ⁹Be = n + ⁸Be(0⁺) cluster configuration in the (⁹Be,⁸Be) reactions channel due the weakly binding energy of neutron on ⁸Be core. A slight events from ⁹Be = α + ⁵He channel are observed in the experimental data, however no events from ⁹Be = n + ⁸Be(2⁺) are measured.
